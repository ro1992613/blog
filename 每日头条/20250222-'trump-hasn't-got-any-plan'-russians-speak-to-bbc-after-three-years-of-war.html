“特朗普没有任何计划”，战争三年后俄罗斯人接受英国广播公司采访时说道。

Recruitment【招募】 posters【海报】 for the invasion of Ukraine are everywhere in Russia

Driving into Tver, the first thing I notice are the soldiers.

They're everywhere. On billboards【广告牌】, the sides of buildings, at bus stops. Portraits with the words "Hero of Russia". Posters of troops with Kalashnikov rifles encouraging the public to "Love, be proud of and defend" Russia.

In other words, to sign up【注册】 and go and fight in Ukraine. Three years after its full-scale invasion of its neighbour, Russia is seeking new recruits.

Despite all the military imagery【军事意象；军事形象；战争景象等】 around town, if you live in Tver it's possible to convince yourself that life is normal. The front line is hundreds of miles away.

"Just look around," Mikhail, a local teacher, tells me. "Cars are passing by and all the shops are open. No shells are falling from anywhere. We are not panicking. We can't hear any sirens【警报声】 wailing【警报声哀号着；警报声长鸣着；警报声呼啸着。】. We do not run to any evacuation【疏散；撤离】 points."

For many Russians their invasion of Ukraine – what the Kremlin still calls a "special military operation" – is something they only encounter on their TV screens.

But for people like Anna, it's much more real.

Some people Anna knew never came back from fighting in Ukraine

"I know a lot of people who went off to fight," Anna says when we get chatting on the street.

"Some of them never came home. I hope [the war] ends as soon as possible."

Donald Trump claims that's what he wants, too. Without inviting Ukraine to the negotiating table, the Trump administration has already entered direct talks with the Russian leadership.

What do Russians make of the US president and his overtures【提议；主动表示；姿态】 to Moscow?

【“overtures” 常见的中文释义为 “提议；主动表示；姿态”。例如：His overtures for peace were well received.（他的和平提议受到了好评。）在这句话中，“overtures” 表示 “提议”，强调某人主动提出的关于和平的建议或主张。再比如：Her overtures of friendship were ignored.（她的友好姿态被忽视了。）这里 “overtures” 表示 “姿态”，即表达友好的一种行为表现。总的来说，“overtures” 通常用来描述某人主动做出的积极行动或表示，以达到某种目的，如建立关系、达成协议等。】

"Trump is a dark horse," believes Anna. "I'm not sure what to expect from him."

'We want Ukraine's total capitulation'【投降；屈服；】

Some of the people I talk to in Tver repeat the official narrative they have been hearing for the last three years on state TV: that their country is not the aggressor, that Russia is defending Russians and Russian-speakers in Ukraine and liberating, not occupying, territory.

It doesn't mean that Russian society as a whole buys into this alternative reality.
这并不意味着俄罗斯社会作为一个整体接受了这种替代现实。

"In a society people always prefer to be in the mainstream," believes Andrei Kolesnikov, a columnist【专栏作家】 for newtimes.ru and Novaya Gazeta. "If the mainstream is pro-war and the TV says that we are at war with the West, the average citizen will think like this. It's easier not to think about the details. You want to live peacefully, so why not join the majority?

"Some researchers call this the foetal【胎儿的；胎的；似胎儿的；未出生的；处于胚胎状态的；未成熟的；幼稚的；软弱无力的；易受影响的；顺从的；】 position. When you defend yourself from this unexplainable world you look like a baby. You say: 'I can't explain to myself what is happening. I believe you. You can feed me with words. I'll accept it.' This is typical for all societies of this kind: a bit [1]authoritarian【专制的；独裁的；威权主义的。】, a bit totalitarian."【专制的；独裁的；极权主义的。】

Larissa and her husband Valery willingly accept the official line.

"We're all for the special military operation," Larissa tells me. "We're ready to volunteer and go there ourselves!"

They haven't yet, clearly.

"We hope [Russia] will be victorious. We want Ukraine's total capitulation."

The police turn up. They've received a call informing them that "suspicious-looking people with a camera" are going around Tver. Meaning us.

Russian police asked the BBC about their reporting in Tver

They're polite but want to know why we're here. They take a statement from our driver. They check our vehicle. They ask me for an official explanation for our visit. I tell them we're gauging【估量；评估；判断】 the mood【情绪；心情；心境；氛围；语气；】 away from Moscow. We show our documents, which are in order.【整齐有序；状况良好】

While we've been talking to the officers, a camera crew【摄制组；全体船员；工作人员】 from Russian state TV has [2]pitched【出现；到来；到达；抵达；（尤指）迅速地从一处移动到另一处；（尤指）突然来到，冒出，出现；（尤指）迅速地从一处移动到另一处；】 up and started filming【拍摄；摄制】 us.

"We were just passing," says the reporter, "and we spotted【发现；看到；注意到；认出；弄脏；弄上斑点；用点装饰；使有污点；使有斑驳；散布；】 the police and recognised you. Can you tell me what's going on?"

"I don't know," I reply. "Perhaps you can tell us?"

"What have you been filming?"

"We've been talking to people on the street," I say. "I believe we're allowed to do that."

"Yes, in our country we have freedom of speech," the reporter replies. "Perhaps the police just want to help you? It's unpleasant to hear Western TV spreading fake news that there's no free speech in Russia. You're talking to people freely and no one gets in your way."

"Apart from you," I point out. "And the police officers standing next to our car."

Russian state TV also interviewed the BBC about what they were doing

The incident【事件】, which lasted about an hour, is no real surprise. Three years of war have fuelled suspicion of the West inside Russia. Early signs of a thaw【解冻；缓和；变得温和】 in US-Russian relations have yet to change that.

From talking to people in Tver it becomes clear that Russians are hoping that an end to the fighting will bring economic relief.

"Prices are so high now for the things I need most," says Yulia as she rocks her baby to sleep in a pram【婴儿车】. "Like the price of potatoes and onions. I really feel it."

But teacher Mikhail doesn't feel that Donald Trump has any strategy for securing peace.

"Unfortunately, Trump hasn't got any plan," believes Mikhail. "He is an improviser.【improviser（即兴表演者；临时凑合者）】 He doesn't know what he's going to do. My sympathies【同情；慰问】 are with him. I'm glad he won. But speaking about this episode, we're all in the dark. And Trump is in the dark himself."

  1. “authoritarian”和“totalitarian”在英语中都与权力的集中和强制有关，但它们之间存在一些细微的区别： ### 含义侧重 - authoritarian：通常用来形容一种强调权威、服从和等级制度的体系或人物。这种体系或人物倾向于要求他人绝对服从其命令和决定，权力往往集中在少数人或一个人手中，但不一定对社会生活的各个方面进行全面的控制。比如，一个公司的老板可能管理风格比较“authoritarian”，他会严格要求员工服从他的决策，但不会过多干涉员工的私人生活和工作以外的事情。 - totalitarian：更加强调对整个社会和个人生活的全面、彻底的控制。极权主义政权试图控制人们生活的方方面面，包括政治、经济、文化、思想、宗教等，不允许任何形式的独立思考和反对意见，追求对社会的绝对统治。如纳粹德国和法西斯意大利，政府对社会的一切都进行严格管控，人们几乎没有任何自由和权利。 ### 控制程度 - authoritarian：控制程度相对较低，主要集中在政治和公共事务等领域，在一定程度上允许社会存在一些私人空间和一定的多样性。例如，一些传统的威权国家，在政治上实行威权统治，但在经济和文化的某些方面还是有一定的自由发展空间。 - totalitarian：控制程度极高，试图消除社会中的一切异质性和多样性，将所有的社会资源和个人行为都纳入到国家的严格控制之下，个人完全服从于国家和政权。 ### 意识形态色彩 - authoritarian：不一定有强烈的、系统的意识形态作为支撑，更多的是基于统治者或统治集团的权力欲望和对秩序的追求来行使权力。 - totalitarian：往往与特定的极端意识形态紧密相连，如纳粹主义、共产主义的极端扭曲形式等，通过宣传和洗脑等手段，使民众接受并服从其意识形态，以实现对社会的全面控制。 ### 举例 - authoritarian：“The authoritarian leader made all the decisions without consulting the public.”（这位威权领导人在不征求公众意见的情况下做出了所有决定。） - totalitarian：“The totalitarian regime imposed strict censorship on all forms of media.”（极权政权对所有形式的媒体实施了严格的审查制度。）↩︎

  2. “pitch up”有以下几种常见的含义及用法： 1. 到达；露面：通常用于口语中，指到达某个地方，有时带有一种突然或意外出现的感觉。例如：We were worried when he didn't pitch up on time.（他没有按时露面，我们很担心。）She finally pitched up at the party at midnight.（她最终在午夜时分出现在聚会上。） 2. 使（声音、音调等）升高：常用来描述声音、乐器音调等的升高。例如：The singer pitched up the final note for a dramatic effect.（歌手为了达到戏剧性的效果把最后一个音的音调升高了。）The radio signal suddenly pitched up, making it hard to understand the message.（无线电信号的音调突然升高，使得信息很难听清。） 3. 搭起（帐篷等）：与“pitch a tent”（搭帐篷）这个短语相关，“pitch up”更加强调动作的过程或开始搭建的动作。例如：They pitched up their tents by the river and started a campfire.（他们在河边搭起帐篷，生起了篝火。）We need to find a good spot to pitch up before it gets dark.（我们需要在天黑前找个好地方搭帐篷。） 4. （车辆等）颠簸着前进：用于描述车辆在不平整的道路等表面上颠簸行驶的状态。例如：The old jeep pitched up and down on the rough mountain road.（那辆旧吉普车在崎岖的山路上颠簸着前进。）The boat pitched up and down in the rough sea.（船在汹涌的海浪中上下颠簸。）↩︎
